This is the fisheye control. Rectilinear is what a normal lens does: it takes the curve out of straight edges, at the cost of stretching faces near the corners - and the wider the field, the harsher that gets. Past about 120 degrees stereographic usually looks better even though it bows a little.

Per shot, saved with the aim. Contrast pivots on a fixed mid-grey and cannot clip either end. Every control here is one the renderer reproduces exactly, so the preview stays honest.

Lens

Properties of the glass, not the shot - one calibration for the whole body, shared by every clip and every segment. Changing them re-maps every angle. These are not a look control: winding them down does flatten the preview, but it is not what the lens saw, and the renderer rejects anything at or under 180 (two lenses cannot meet below that) - so the file would come back different from the preview. To reduce the fisheye look use the output projection below, or a narrower field of view.
